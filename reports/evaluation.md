# SiteScout evaluation: retrospective plausibility test

Everything below is a **retrospective plausibility test**, generated by `scripts/evaluate.py` from the pipeline's own outputs. It checks whether the ranking is plausible against the few public chargers that exist; it is not a measure of how right the ranking is, and the ranking is not ground truth.

## A. Backtest

Backtest mode removes every existing charger from every feature before scoring (SPEC §5). A **hit** is a candidate within 1000 m of a known charging site.

- Known charging sites: **5** (OSM charging stations merged within 50 m; manual charger list: missing).
- Hit candidates: **5** of 300. SiteScout's top 30 holds 4 of them.

Precision and Recall are measured over the **candidate ranking**: a hit is a candidate, not a charging site. Recall@30 is the share of all hit candidates (candidates within 1000 m of a known charging site) that rank in the top 30. The number of hit candidates and the number of distinct known charging sites can differ: several candidates can lie near the same site, and a site can have no candidate near it.

| Metric | SiteScout [95% interval] | Population-only (`pop_5km`) [95% interval] | Random (mean of 1000 seeds) | SiteScout − population [95% interval] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Precision@10 | 0.100 [0.000, 0.300] | 0.100 [0.000, 0.300] | 0.016 | [-0.200, 0.300] |
| Precision@20 | 0.150 [0.000, 0.300] | 0.050 [0.000, 0.200] | 0.017 | [-0.100, 0.250] |
| Precision@30 | 0.133 [0.033, 0.267] | 0.067 [0.000, 0.200] | 0.017 | [0.000, 0.167] |
| Recall@30 | 0.800 [0.333, 1.000] | 0.400 [0.000, 1.000] | 0.104 | [0.000, 1.000] |

SiteScout beats the population-only baseline on Precision@30 (0.133 against 0.067). The 95% bootstrap interval of the difference, [0.000, 0.167], includes 0, so the data cannot tell the two apart.

**Read with care.** With 5 hit candidates, one hit more or less moves Precision@30 by 0.033; the intervals are wide and no strong claim follows. In backtest mode the charging-gap component is the same for every candidate (SPEC §5), so this tests the other four components. The OSM data postdates the chargers, so mapping around them may still carry a trace of them.

## C. Network effect

Production mode. 108 candidates are eligible: production score in the top half (percentile at least 50) and a host. Demand: modelled population (WorldPop) summed into 4094 H3 cells at resolution 7, normalised to 1, with demand within 10 km of the 5 known charging sites weighted by 0.5. A site covers demand within 10 km; selected sites are at least 2 km apart (except in Top-30, which takes scores only); λ = 0.01.

- Exact MCLP solver status: **Optimal** (Optimal Solution Found).
- Exact-versus-greedy gap: **0.64%**, measured on the optimization objective Σ wᵢ·yᵢ + λ·Σ sⱼ·xⱼ (0.6635 against 0.6592), which includes the λ score term. It is not the difference between the population-coverage percentages above.
- Overlap: MCLP and greedy share 28 sites, MCLP and Top-30 5, greedy and Top-30 7.

Selecting sites together covers more of the country than taking the best-scoring sites one by one, which cluster where scores are highest. Coverage is modelled population within a radius: it says nothing about grid capacity, land or charging demand.

## F. Radius robustness and a stronger baseline

All methods choose from the same 108 eligible sites with site scores fixed and only the coverage radius changing; the spaced baselines take the highest scores while keeping every pair of sites at least their spacing apart. At 10 km Optimized covers 49.8% of modelled population; the strongest baseline, top by score, at least 10 km apart, covers 46.6% but reaches only 27 of 30 sites, a gap of 3.2 percentage points, while the strongest baseline with all 30 sites, top by score, at least 5 km apart, covers 40.9% (8.9 points behind); the unspaced Top-30 covers 24.0%. Across 5 km to 15 km the gap to the strongest baseline runs from 2.0 points at 15 km to 4.8 points at 7.5 km (against the best baseline that reaches all 30 sites: 2.7 to 11.9 points), and Optimized covers more than every baseline at every radius. Letting the zone around the 5 known chargers, where demand is down-weighted, follow the radius instead of staying at 10 km, as the sensitivity table in section C does, changes the selected sites at 5 km (4 of 30), 7.5 km (3 of 30), 15 km (1 of 30) and moves Optimized's coverage by at most 0.4 points (26.3% against 25.9% at 5 km), which is why the two tables differ slightly. At 10 km the three shipped selections are reproduced exactly (checked against network.json).
